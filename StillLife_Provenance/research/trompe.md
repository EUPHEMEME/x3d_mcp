# trompe — Gijsbrechts, *Trompe l'oeil. Board Partition with Letter Rack and Music Book*, 1668

SMK Copenhagen **KMS3059**. Oil on canvas, **123.5 × 107 cm** (net; framed 128.2 × 111.9 × 6 cm).
Signed on the painted landscape print: **"C. N. Gysbrechts. F. Ao 1668"**.
Painted for Frederik III's court; hung in the **Perspective Chamber of the Royal Kunstkammer**
alongside ~21 other Gijsbrechts deceptions. Documented working rule of that commission:
**objects painted at natural size (1:1)**. Model everything life-size and keep it life-size.

Primary visual source used here: SMK Open IIIF master, 6350 × 7235 px
(`https://iip.smk.dk/iiif/jp2/r494vp637_KMS3059-cropped.tif.jp2`) — ≈ **59.3 px/cm**,
inspected at full res plus six detail crops. Positions below were measured off that image
and are given as **(x, y) fractions of canvas width/height**, origin top-left, plus cm
where useful. Literature anchors: Koester, *Illusions: Gijsbrechts* (SMK 1999) cat. 9;
Koester, *Painted Illusions* (2000) cat. 1; Wahrman, *Mr. Collier's Letter Racks* (2012)
pp. 31–32, fig. 2.1; Mauritshuis, *The Eye Deceived* (2005).

---

## 0. THE GOVERNING FACT

This is not a scene. It is a **flat pine plank wall, dead frontal, with nothing standing
more than a hand's breadth off it**. There is no floor, no horizon, no room, no
perspective recession anywhere. Every illusion of depth is carried by four things only:

1. **overlap** (paper over tape over board; curtain over everything),
2. **cast shadow** from one raking light,
3. **paper behavior** — folds, dog-ears, curl lifting corners 1–3 cm off the plane,
4. **frame-breaking at the board's own edges** (not the canvas edges).

**Depth budget (hard ceiling): 10 cm.** Board plane = z 0. Tape ≈ 0.3 cm. Flat letters
0.3–1 cm. Curling corners to 3 cm. Music book, letter-rosette, hanging penner/ink set
4–6 cm. Curtain bellies 8–10 cm max. If any camera angle reveals more depth than this,
or any object reads as free-standing, the picture has failed its own genre.

Camera for our version: axis **perpendicular to the board**, eye height at canvas
center, **narrow FOV (~20–25°)** so plank edges stay parallel and the rod does not
converge. The painting has effectively zero vanishing-point drama; do not add any.

## 1. THE SUPPORT STRUCTURE (the "picture plane" objects)

### 1.1 The board partition
- **Vertical softwood planks** (deal — pine/spruce), 5 planks visible, each ≈ **17–22 cm
  wide**, butt-jointed; joints read as thin dark vertical lines. Unpainted, scrubbed
  pale wood: warm cream-buff (light passages ≈ `#d6c9a3`–`#bed69f` cast), grain lines
  grey-brown, occasional knots and rubbed stains. **Matte, roughness 0.75–0.85**, faint
  anisotropy along the vertical grain. No varnish sheen.
- The board is **complete inside the canvas**: a near-black surround (`#191909`,
  `#140604`) frames it on all four sides. Board edges: left ≈ x 0.055, top ≈ y 0.06,
  bottom rail ≈ y 0.925; right edge swallowed by the curtain.
- Along the top and bottom black margins runs a **row of small gilt tack heads** —
  dotted lines of ~0.5 cm dome tacks, the only thing articulating the black. Keep them:
  they declare the black to be a *thing* (leather/cloth-faced wall), not empty space.
- A horizontal **batten** runs behind the board's top edge: letters and pamphlets are
  shoved **behind the board top**, leaning out over it against the black (cluster
  spanning x 0.38–0.75, y 0.01–0.09). This is the top frame-break.

### 1.2 The red tape grid (the letter rack proper)
- Not parallel straps (that is Collier's later formula) — a **full grid** of narrow
  red-orange woven tape (linen tape, vermilion, ≈ **1.5–2 cm wide**), matte fabric,
  roughness ~0.8, slight twist and visible slack sag between fixings.
- Layout ≈ 3 columns × 4 rows over the exposed board: verticals near x 0.125, 0.365,
  0.60; horizontals near y 0.115 (just under the rod), 0.505, 0.72; at the bottom
  corners the tape runs **diagonally** into the corners (bottom-left crossing pinned at
  ≈ (0.10, 0.885)).
- Every crossing is fixed with a **round dome-head iron nail** (~0.8 cm, dark steel,
  small sharp specular). Tapes bow 0.5–1 cm where letters are stuffed under them.
  Nothing tucked under tape sits parallel to it — every paper is rotated 5–35°.

### 1.3 The iron curtain rod + rings
- Wrought-iron rod, ≈ **0.8–1 cm diameter**, near-black with one thin specular line
  along its top; runs the full width at y ≈ 0.075–0.085 and **visibly sags** a few mm
  mid-span; left end is cranked down. ~25 small iron **rings** (~2 cm) carry the curtain.

### 1.4 The curtain — the deception inside the deception
- The Parrhasius gambit: a curtain a courtier would reach to draw aside. It covers the
  **entire right half**: from x ≈ 0.36 at the rod, its leading edge sweeps down-right to
  ≈ (0.86, 0.95). That leading edge is **the dominant diagonal of the picture**.
- Material: heavy **silk** (taffeta-weight), very dark **green with a blue-grey cast**;
  fold ridges catch broad soft grey-green speculars, fold cores go to near-black.
  A narrow **gold-yellow selvage/trim** runs along the leading edge and hem — the only
  warm metallic accent in the picture. Roughness ~0.45–0.55, anisotropic; deep folds
  gathered into one big mass at the right (bellies 8–10 cm off the plane).
- It hangs **in front of** the board on its rod: it must cast a soft broad shadow onto
  the board to the lower-left of its leading edge. In the painting that shadow is a
  1–4 cm warm dark band hugging the edge.

## 2. THE PROPS — object by object

Everything below hangs on, is tucked under, or is pinned to the plane. Light-relief
inventory, left column → center → bottom, with measured positions.

### 2.1 Comb case ("kamfoder") — the wall pocket, left column
- Occupies x 0.04–0.22, y 0.09–0.62 — a tall tiered **wall pocket of crimson silk
  velvet** (deep wine `#4a1520`-ish in shadow, glowing red on lit fold edges; velvet =
  absorptive body + bright nap edge sheen), bound and cross-strapped with **silver-grey
  galloon tape** ~1.5 cm wide (woven metallic braid, low-gloss specular), nailed to the
  board, pointed/gabled top. **6–7 pocket tiers**, each pocket sagging open.
- Contents, top to bottom: a **dark horn comb** (diagonal, striped translucency) in the
  upper tier; a small **white book/box** on edge; a **white goose-quill pen** (~28 cm,
  full feather) thrust in at ~35°, pointing up-right, its shadow raking right across the
  board; folded white papers; a **boxwood comb** (honey-tan, ~10 × 6 cm, fine teeth on
  one edge) leaning out at lower-left; a letterpress **almanac page** ("…anno 1668",
  black + red printing) folded over a pocket lip; a second dark wide-toothed comb below.
- A **rolled German calendar** hangs from a lower tier at (0.19, 0.45): title page
  rolled into a cylinder, blackletter title readable as *"Alter und Neuer Zeit- und
  [Schreib-]Calender … M DC LXVIII"* — black + red letterpress with a woodcut frieze of
  tiny figures along the top.
- From the case's lowest pocket hangs the **great seal pendant** at (0.16, 0.55): a
  **red wax seal ~6–7 cm diameter** in a paper/parchment boat, embossed (arms visible as
  relief), hanging by a parchment tail — glossy vermilion-to-dark-red wax, roughness
  ~0.25, the biggest single red mass in the picture.
- A long **dark blue-green cord** drops from the top-left corner down the whole left
  side, ending in a **silk tassel** at (0.10, 0.60). It hangs dead vertical =
  the plumb line that proves the wall is upright.

### 2.2 Music book — lower left, the title prop
- Center ≈ (0.155, 0.635), spanning x 0.06–0.27. An **oblong (landscape) manuscript
  music book, ~16 × 21 cm**, limp paper cover, ~20+ leaves, tucked spine-down under the
  horizontal tape so it hangs open facing the viewer.
- The front leaves are **rolled back into a scroll** at its upper-left corner (a full
  360° paper curl, ~4 cm diameter) — the single most bravura piece of paper-handling in
  the painting. Facing pages show **hand-ruled staves** (8–9 systems/page, slightly wavy
  ink lines) with dense handwritten notation; a grey-blue paper-bound book and a brown
  board are stacked behind it, edges visible.
- Its cast shadow drops **down-right ~2–3 cm**, soft-edged.

### 2.3 The letter rosette on its file — bottom center-left
- Center ≈ (0.35, 0.815), overall Ø ≈ 25–28 cm. A **letter file**: an iron spike/pin
  driven into the board, skewering **a dozen-plus folded letters and pamphlets** that
  fan into a rosette. Mostly manuscript letters (iron-gall brown-black script), one
  letterpress pamphlet page (dense black text) mixed in. Individual packets ≈ 9 × 12 to
  11 × 16 cm, each folded letter shown **edge-on** (paper thickness visible), corners
  dog-eared. Deepest paper stack in the picture: ~5–6 cm proud of the board.
- Its lowest sheets **hang below the board's bottom rail**, overlapping the black margin
  (to y ≈ 0.94) — the bottom frame-break, mirrored by a single manuscript letter
  hanging over the rail at (0.165, 0.937).

### 2.4 The landscape print + pamphlet stack — center, the signature carrier
- Center ≈ (0.475, 0.775), sheet ≈ 13 × 17 cm on a ~2 cm stack of stitched pamphlets,
  the whole tucked under tape. A monochrome **etching**: grey-brown wooded landscape,
  two tiny staffage figures, white margin, inscribed in the plate margin
  **"C.N. Gijsbrechts F. A° 1668"** — the painting's actual signature. Reproduce this
  as the provenance-bearing object in-scene: it is where the artist put his own.
- Directly above at (0.475, 0.665): the **Oprechte Haerlemse Courant** — Haarlem
  newspaper, blackletter masthead + "SPANGIEN" column heading, two-column letterpress,
  top edge curling forward.

### 2.5 Writing kit — hanging hardware, center-right
- From a dome nail at (0.60, 0.72) hang, on **red cords with red pom/wax knots**:
  - a squat **dark-glass ink bottle** (~7 cm, near-black green glass, strong smooth
    speculars, roughness ~0.15),
  - a **penner** — turned cylindrical **black leather pen case ~15–18 cm** (dull gloss,
    roughness ~0.35),
  - a second black leather **quill holder** at (0.655, 0.83) with **white quill butts
    fanning out of its top**.
  These are the deepest hard objects (~4–5 cm standoff); they must hang **plumb** and
  each throws a distinct soft shadow down-right.
- **Sealing-wax sticks**: two vermilion shellac sticks (~9–10 cm × 1.2 cm, glossy,
  snapped/charred ends) tucked behind tape at ≈ (0.39, 0.65) (diagonal) and near the
  print's top-left corner.
- A slender black **penknife/stylus** (~18 cm, blade up) thrust behind the tape at
  ≈ (0.24, 0.72–0.80), pointing up-right at ~55° — a hard dark diagonal accent.

### 2.6 Newspapers & almanacs — bottom-left literacy cluster
- **"The Lond[on Gazette]"** at (0.09, 0.795): English newspaper, roman-type masthead,
  folded, tucked under the diagonal tape, big dog-ear; a small sealed letter (red wax
  blob ~2 cm) pinned over it at (0.075, 0.84).
- **"Almanach"** title page at (0.19, 0.87): red + black letterpress, red seal print.
- The bottom-left corner in general: paper + red tape + wax = the densest incident
  cluster; it balances the curtain mass diagonally.

### 2.7 Letters everywhere (the connective tissue)
- ~25–30 letters distributed over the exposed board and behind the top batten. Material:
  **laid rag paper**, warm white → grey-buff, always shown as **folded packets** with
  visible thickness, address panels in brown iron-gall script, many with **red wax
  seals** (~2–2.5 cm glossy blobs, some broken in half). Several letters are only
  **half-revealed by the curtain edge** — cut by cloth, not by the frame.
- Paper optics: matte (roughness 0.85), but slightly translucent at raking angles;
  edges and dog-ears catch light as bright lines. **No letter lies flat**: every one
  bows, curls, or lifts a corner. Flat rectangles are the tell of a fake.

## 3. THE PICTURE — construction

- **Horizon: none.** Frontal parallel projection; eye level = canvas center. The wall
  IS the picture plane. All compositional structure is 2D pattern + shallow shadow.
- **Dominant diagonal:** the curtain's leading edge, upper-center → lower-right
  ((0.36, 0.05) → (0.86, 0.95)). **Counter-diagonal:** the object-density band rising
  from the rosette/Gazette cluster at bottom-left through music book → seal pendant →
  calendar roll → comb-case top at upper-left. The two diagonals form an X whose
  crossing sits just left of the ink-bottle nail — near, not at, canvas center.
- **The revealed field is a triangle** (wide at bottom-left, apex at top-center where
  curtain meets rod). Object clusters live in that triangle; the curtain half is
  almost empty of incident — one dark breathing mass against the busy half.
- **Nearest-to-viewer:** the curtain bellies, then the letter rosette and the hanging
  ink/penner set. They lead the eye by shadow depth, not by scale.
- **Light:** enters **upper left**, elevation ~35–40°, azimuth ~40° from the plane —
  raking enough that 2 cm of relief throws a 3–5 cm shadow **down-and-right**. Moderately
  hard: shadow cores are definite but every edge has 1–2 cm of penumbra. Color: warm,
  slightly yellow; shadows are **warm umber glazes, never grey/black** — in the paint
  they look almost scorched (soft brown smoke around each object). Since X_ITE ignores
  `light.shadows`, ALL of this must be baked via `contact()`-style geometry/texture:
  one consistent shadow vector (offset down-right ≈ 1.5–2.5× standoff), umber-tinted
  multiply, soft edge.
- **What the light leaves dark:** the entire surround, the curtain's fold cores, the
  velvet pocket interiors, and the underside of every curl. The board itself darkens
  toward its edges (subtle corner falloff — bake a gentle vignette into the board
  texture, brightest zone center-left where the paper cluster lives).
- **Tonal key: low.** Values — surround ≈ 5%, curtain body 8–15% with 30% ridge lights,
  board 45–60%, papers 75–88% (the brightest things in the picture; nothing reaches
  white). Reds sit ~35–50% value but maximum chroma: the **red system** (tape grid +
  seals + wax sticks + rubricated titles + hanging cords) is one distributed accent
  network that moves the eye through the triangle. Gold exists only twice: tack heads
  and curtain trim.
- **Cropping / frame-breaks:** nothing is cut by the canvas edge. All frame-breaking is
  **internal**: letters lean out over the board's top edge; the rosette and one letter
  hang past the bottom rail; the curtain swallows the right edge. That is precisely
  what makes it read as an *object on a wall* rather than a window — our X3D version
  must keep the full board silhouette inside frame, with a true black surround, and
  break the board's edges, not the viewport's.
- **Scale check:** at 1:1, canvas 107 × 123.5 cm ⇒ a letter packet ≈ 1/10 canvas width.
  If letters render bigger than that relative to frame, the wall shrinks to a prop.

## 4. FAILURE CONDITIONS (from the genre, for the harness)

1. Any visible floor/ceiling/side wall, or plank convergence ⇒ failed (it became a room).
2. Object standoff > ~10 cm or a shadow longer than ~3× standoff ⇒ failed.
3. Two shadow directions ⇒ failed (single raking source, upper left, is the whole engine).
4. A flat, uncurled, unrotated paper rectangle ⇒ reads instantly as CG.
5. Curtain lighter than the board, or without its cast shadow band ⇒ the Parrhasius
   joke dies.
6. Neutral grey shadows ⇒ wrong century. Umber, warm, soft-edged.
