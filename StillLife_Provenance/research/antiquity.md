# antiquity — MANN inv. 8611: the Julia Felix glass bowl of fruit

**Pompeian Fourth Style xenia. Praedia of Julia Felix (Pompeii II.4.3), tablinum 92,
north wall. Excavated 6 July 1755 (the frieze was cut from the wall with the entire
north wall). Museo Archeologico Nazionale di Napoli, inv. 8611. c. AD 62–79.**

Dimensions: the displayed slab is **234 × 100 cm** and carries TWO scenes plus fictive
framing; the famous glass-bowl scene alone is catalogued at **114 × 74 cm** (some
sources 108 × 70). Its companion scene on the same slab, to the left: a dead game
bird slumped over a shelf edge above a **blue glass stemmed goblet** — keep this in
mind, it proves the painter's glass vocabulary is deliberate, not a one-off.

This panel is the standard citation for *the earliest convincing depiction of
transparent glass in Western painting*. Everything below is measured from three
photographs (see §4 white-balance warning) plus catalog data.

---

## 1. THE PROPS — each object, actually

### 1.1 The glass bowl ("glass krater")
- **Form:** deep bowl, hemispherical belly narrowing to a **low pedestal foot** with
  a splayed pad; wide **flaring lip**. No handles. Painted width ≈ 0.40 of panel
  width ≈ **45 cm as painted** — roughly life-size-plus; the real vessel type is a
  blown table bowl of ~25–35 cm diameter. Catalogs call it a *cratere di vetro*.
- **Material:** mid-1st-c. AD Roman soda-lime-silica glass, **free-blown,
  thin-walled (≈1.5–3 mm)**, fire-rounded rim. Naturally tinted **pale blue-green
  from iron** in the sand (the "RNCBGY" family); antimony-decolourized colorless
  glass was just becoming fashionable at exactly this date. Fine **pinprick seed
  bubbles**; faint horizontal tooling lines. No cut decoration, no prunts, no ribs.
- **Optics:** IOR ≈ 1.52, high transmission with a slight blue-green cast
  (transmission tint ≈ rgb 0.72, 0.80, 0.78), gloss roughness ≈ 0.03–0.08. Thick
  only at rim and foot, where the tint doubles up and reads darker.
- **How the painter renders it — copy these four devices, they ARE the picture:**
  1. **Background shows through** the empty glass above the fruit fill-line, only
     slightly greyed (≈10% darker than the bare wall).
  2. **Fruit behind the glass wall is 20–30% darker, desaturated, and
     soft-edged** relative to identical fruit above the rim. This darken+blur is
     the entire transparency illusion.
  3. **Both rim edges are drawn** — front ellipse and back ellipse as darker
     slate-teal contour lines (#3c4a4a, darkest strokes #2e3838). The double line
     is the "thickness" read.
  4. **Speculars are discrete white dashes, not gradients:** short warm-white
     (#e8e4d8) ticks along the front-left rim, plus ONE long diagonal streak down
     the left belly. Nothing else is shiny.
- Builder note: if true transmission compositing is unreliable in X_ITE, the
  painter's own method fakes perfectly in 3D: glass shell at alpha ≈ 0.25 with the
  teal tint + a separately-darkened "fruit behind glass" material + geometry or
  texture lines for the two rim ellipses + a highlight streak. Verify by eye that
  interior fruit reads darker than rim-top fruit.

### 1.2 The fruit in the bowl
- **Apples and quinces**, yellow with red-brown blush, some green-white; piled
  ABOVE the rim (lit, saturated) and visible THROUGH the wall (darkened, see 1.1).
  Body color lit ≈ #c9a544, blush ≈ #9d5a3a, pale ones ≈ #b7b083. Satin surface
  (roughness ≈ 0.4), tiny single specular dot each.
- **A double bunch of grapes** draped over the RIGHT rim, spilling down the outside
  of the bowl — dusky **red-brown/amber grapes** (#6e4738), each berry rimmed with a
  pale top light dot (#c49a6a). Not glossy purple supermarket grapes.
- **A dry vine twig** rises from the bunch above everything else — a thin dark
  red-brown forked line, the highest element in the panel (reaches y ≈ 0.05). This
  little scribble breaks the dead band of wall at the top; keep it.
- One catalog description (De Caro): "colmo di mele e di uva, con un bel doppio
  grappolo" — full of apples and grapes, with a fine double bunch.

### 1.3 The burst pomegranate (on the ledge, lower left)
- Sits directly on the ledge at the LEFT frame edge, split open: maroon-red skin
  (#7a3b3a), seed mass (#a04a48), pale flesh (#c9a37c), with juice/debris trailing
  left until the fictive frame cuts it. It is the nearest, lowest, most broken
  object — the memento at the picture's entry point.

### 1.4 The leaning amphora
- Small buff-clay table amphora (an *urceus*-scale vessel, not a shipping amphora):
  ovoid body, short neck, **two loop handles** shoulder-to-rim, blunt toe. As
  painted ≈ 45–50 cm long. Captions disagree on contents (wine jar / garum jar);
  ancientrome.ru reads it as **sealed, tied with cordage**. Model it stoppered.
- **Material:** pale yellow-buff Campanian clay, unslipped, matte and slightly
  gritty — roughness 0.7–0.85, zero specular. Lit belly ≈ #c8a95f, shadow side
  ≈ #8a6b35, contours drawn in red-brown.
- **Pose:** leans at ≈ **30° off vertical**, mouth up-right, toe down-left resting
  on the main ledge beside the quince; the shoulder is propped on the step corner.
  The balance is frankly improbable — the diagonal is manufactured to bridge the
  two ledge levels. Do not "fix" it.

### 1.5 The quince at the amphora's toe
- One yellow quince/apple (#c9a544) alone on the ledge at panel centre-bottom
  (0.525, 0.665), radius ≈ 0.045 panel-widths. It is the hinge of the whole
  composition (see §2). Small soft shadow to its right.

### 1.6 The olla of dried fruit
- Wheel-thrown **coarse-ware olla** (cooking/storage pot): squat, round-bellied,
  everted rim, orange-red clay, matte (roughness ≈ 0.7). Painted dia ≈ 0.155
  panel-widths ≈ **18 cm**, rim at y 0.52, base y 0.79. De Caro: the vessel forms
  match real excavated Pompeian specimens exactly — model a real olla, not a
  generic pot. Lit ≈ #b97f5f, shadow ≈ #75574e, rim interior dark.
- **Heaped over the rim with dried prunes** (De Caro; file captions say raisins or
  Syrian figs — dried dark fruit either way): #6c4736 with #a97a4e top lights,
  matte wrinkled lumps, mounded to y ≈ 0.45.
- An **ovoid terracotta lid** (alternative reading: a pair of large dried figs)
  leans against the olla's front-left, from (0.70, 0.60) down to (0.76, 0.79).

### 1.7 The ledge (it is stone, and it steps)
- A dressed-stone plinth in TWO levels: main block on the left, and a **lower,
  recessed shelf** on the right carrying the olla. Grey stone with warm mottle —
  NOT wood, no grain. Lit top ≈ #b9b3a6, front face ≈ #7d6a62, the step's
  right-facing shadow face ≈ #5d4948 (one of the darkest areas in the picture).
- Main block front-top edge at y = 0.755 spanning x 0.03 → 0.66; visible top-surface
  band only 0.70–0.755 deep (≈5° viewing angle). Step corner at x ≈ 0.60–0.66;
  right shelf top y ≈ 0.80, its front edge y ≈ 0.86.

### 1.8 The wall and the fictive frame
- Behind everything, CLOSE: a plaster wall in **pale warm grey-white with faint
  green-grey mottling** (#d5d2c9 in the lit upper-left, falling to grey-olive
  #8c8379 in the upper-right corner). The wall **lightens and warms toward the
  ledge** — strongest in the recess behind amphora and olla (toward #c0a37b) — a
  luminous halo under the objects. No cornice, no window, no void.
- The scene is bordered by a **fictive frame**: maroon-purple band (#5f3a3c),
  ~0.025 panel-widths on the sides, ~0.06 panel-heights top, with a **teal-blue
  fillet** (#4f7a80) and a thin pale inner line. Below the whole frieze runs a
  stucco egg-and-dart. Build the frame in-scene: it is what turns the arrangement
  into a picture, and it is a legitimate provenance surface.
- Fresco fabric: buon fresco on polished lime (marble-dust *politiones*), details
  a secco; paint layers 0.01–0.10 mm. Pigments: yellow + red ochre, green earth,
  Egyptian blue (the glass and blue fillets), carbon black, lime white. Surface is
  **low-sheen satin, not matte-dead** (roughness ≈ 0.5 with a faint broad
  specular), carrying hairline craquelure and plaster losses along the 1755 cut
  edges. Mottle is mandatory; craquelure optional.

## 2. THE PICTURE — construction, with numbers

Coordinates are fractions of the FULL panel including border, x left→right,
y top→bottom. Panel aspect W:H = 114:74 ≈ 1.54.

| element | position |
|---|---|
| glass bowl rim ellipse | major axis x 0.105 → 0.50, at y ≈ 0.28 (left) – 0.33 (right tip slightly lower) |
| bowl back-rim top / front-rim bottom | y 0.20 / y 0.38 → ellipse openness ≈ 0.30 |
| bowl foot | (0.30–0.42, 0.68–0.72); total bowl height ≈ 0.52 panel-heights |
| grape bunch | x 0.35–0.56, y 0.13–0.42, over the right rim |
| vine twig apex | (0.40–0.52, 0.05) — highest mark in the panel |
| pomegranate | centre (0.145, 0.66), debris trailing to the left frame edge |
| quince | centre (0.525, 0.665) |
| amphora axis | (0.70, 0.24) → (0.55, 0.63); ≈ 30° off vertical |
| olla | centre-x 0.83, rim y 0.52, base y 0.79, prune heap to y 0.45 |
| main ledge front-top edge | y 0.755, x 0.03 → 0.66 |
| right shelf top / front edge | y ≈ 0.80 / y ≈ 0.86 |

**Horizon / eye level — deliberately inconsistent, and you must keep it.** The rim
ellipse is open at ratio ≈ 0.30 (≈ 17° above the rim plane: you look INTO the bowl
and see the fruit inside) while the ledge top shows only a ~5° sliver. Roman
empirical perspective uses a different viewpoint per object. In 3D: camera at
ledge-top height + ~5°, long lens, frontal; then **tilt the bowl (or its rim
construction) ~10–12° toward camera** to open the ellipse. Do not let a consistent
camera flatten the bowl — a "correct" view kills the resemblance instantly.

**Dominant diagonal:** the amphora, upper-right → lower-left, ≈ 30° off vertical.
It is countered by the ledge stepping DOWN left → right, and echoed by the grape
spill down the bowl's right flank. The three together make a broad X centred just
right of the quince.

**Eye path:** enter at the burst pomegranate (front-left, nearest, at the crop) →
up the bowl mass → across the fruit pile → down the grape spill → down the amphora
diagonal → land on the lone quince → step down-right to the olla → out. Nearest
the viewer: pomegranate, bowl front rim, quince — all pushed against the ledge's
front edge.

**Cropping:** the pomegranate group is cut by the left frame; the frieze visibly
continues beyond both sides (companion game-bird scene at left). The panel is a
window onto a longer shelf, not a complete world. Compose so the left frame
genuinely amputates something.

**Light:** one soft key from the viewer's LEFT, slightly frontal — azimuth
≈ 250–260°, elevation ≈ 35–45° by the modelling; warm-neutral in colour. But cast
shadows are **conventional understatement**: short warm-brown tongues to the
RIGHT, ≤ 0.4 × object height, soft-edged (bowl's shadow: x 0.42 → 0.57 at
y 0.68–0.72; small ellipse right of the quince; the lid shadows the olla). Treat
them as baked contact occlusion plus a short rightward smear — never a geometric
projected silhouette. X_ITE ignores light.shadows anyway; here that limitation is
period-accurate.

**What stays dark:** the step's right-facing face, the bowl's foot/underside, the
right flank of every vessel, the maroon border. **What stays light:** the wall
band above the ledge, the glass speculars, the top-lit fruit.

**Tonal key (measured, 8-bit luminance of the museum photo):** min 9, p5 70,
median 118, p95 157, max 187; mean 116, σ 27. That is a **compressed mid-key**:
no true blacks outside the border, NOTHING above ~0.73 white. For the render:
target mean ≈ 0.45, clamp highlights ≈ 0.75, floor shadows ≈ 0.27. If the render
shows a full 0–1 histogram it does not look like this fresco.

## 3. Series context (one paragraph, for provenance text)

The panel is one scene of a provisions frieze that ran around tablinum 92 of Julia
Felix's rental estate — companion panels from the praedia show bread, fish, eggs
with hung quail/thrushes and silver vessels, and the famous coins-and-writing-kit.
The genre is *xenia* — hospitality gifts to guests, after the Greek tradition
(Vitruvius De arch. 6.7.4). On this slab: game bird + blue glass goblet (left),
glass bowl of fruit + amphora + olla (right). Found 6 July 1755, among the first
paintings cut for the Bourbon royal collection → MANN.

## 4. White-balance warning (three photos measured)

The same wall reads **saturated ochre** in the tungsten museum photo (#a88e6b),
**turquoise** in a cool daylight photo (#8fccd7), and **pale warm grey-white**
(#d6d5d3 lit / #8c8379 shadow) in the best-balanced photo. The palette in §1 is
reconciled toward the third. Do NOT build the wall as saturated yellow ochre, and
do not build it cyan. The ground is a quiet greyed plaster; the WARMTH lives in
the objects (ochre amphora, red olla, fruit) and in the halo near the ledge.
